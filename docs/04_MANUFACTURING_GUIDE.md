# Manufacturing Guide — Caramel Shell 2S (Rev B)

Written for a workshop supervisor.
- Part names follow `03_COMPONENT_SCHEDULE_AND_BOM.md` and the Blender object names.
- General tolerances unless stated otherwise:
  - Machined timber: ±0.5 mm.
  - Frame assembly: ±1.5 mm overall, diagonals within 2 mm.
  - Foam cutting: ±2 mm.
  - Sewing: ±3 mm seam allowance.

| # | Stage | Materials | Tools / machines | Key measurements and technique | Common mistakes | QC check |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Prepare technical drawings | — | Blender file, `drawings/`, CAD/CAM | Export rib, former and panel profiles from Blender (select the object → File › Export › SVG/DXF via an add-on, or use top/side ortho renders) to the CNC CAM. Print a 1:1 rib template | Using the presentation scale instead of 1:1 | Template matches the model to ±1 mm |
| 2 | Select and inspect timber | Beech, birch ply | Moisture meter | Beech 8–12 % MC; straight grain (slope ≤ 1:12 in rails); no knots, shakes or decay in rails. Ply EN 636-2, no voids on edges | Wet timber leads to loose joints later | Record MC per batch |
| 3 | Cut frame components | Beech | Crosscut saw, planer, thicknesser, table saw | Rails: front 38 × 120 × 1525, rear 38 × 86 × 1525, sides 38 × 120 × 578, stretcher 32 × 70 × 522 + tenons, cross members 32 × 50 × 522 | Cutting rails to arm-outside length (the model rails stop at the inner arm panels) | Check every part against the cut list |
| 4 | Prepare curved components | 18 birch ply; beech or 3 × 9 mm ply laminations; 5 mm bending ply | CNC router; vacuum or press laminating forms | CNC ribs (6), back formers (4), corner formers (2), inner arm panels (2, leaning profile). Corner portions of the continuous rails are bent-laminated on a form (R ≈ 120–250) and cured ≥ 4 h | Springback on laminations: overbend the form 3–5 % | Ribs on a template ±1 mm; laminations within 2 mm of the form |
| 5 | Machine joints | — | Dowel borer, mortiser, drill jig, CNC | Rail ends: 2 × Ø10 dowel holes (depth 28 in the rail, 12 in the panel), M8 bolt hole Ø9 along the axis, Ø10 cross-dowel hole 32 from the end. Stretcher mortises 24 × 50 × 25 deep. Rib half-lap notches 28 × 14 for the stringers | Misaligned dowel holes; cross-dowel off the bolt axis | Dry-fit every joint before gluing |
| 6 | Assemble the wooden skeleton | PVAc D3, dowels, M8 bolts, screws | Frame clamps, assembly table / jig | Order: (a) seat box (front/rear rails ↔ inner arm panels + side rails, stretcher, cross members) on a flat table; (b) arm ribs onto the panels; (c) back formers on the back base rail; (d) continuous stringers into the rib notches; (e) corner formers | Gluing too much at once; open time of PVAc ≈ 10–15 min | Joint lines closed, no gaps > 0.2 mm |
| 7 | Glue and reinforce the frame | Corner blocks, stretcher blocks, screws 4.5 × 50 | Drill/driver | 4 corner blocks + 4 stretcher blocks glued + screwed. Bolts torqued to ≈ 8 N·m after the glue sets | Over-torquing crushes the ply face | Bolt marks painted after torque |
| 8 | Check dimensions and squareness | — | Tape, square, straightedge | Overall 1900 × 900 ± 2; seat-box diagonals equal ± 2; frame sits flat on the table (≤ 1 mm rock); arm rims symmetric ± 3 | Measuring before the glue is fully set | Record the diagonals on the frame card |
| 9 | Install leg mounting blocks | Beech blocks, M8 inserts | Drill press, insert driver | Insert axis square to the block face ±1°. Front leg centres (±870, −310), rear (±800, +290), centre foot (0, +190) | Insert proud of the face, so the leg rocks | Test-fit a leg; no wobble |
| 10 | Install suspension rails | — | — | The front/rear rail tops are the spring rails; clip lines y = −311 / +249 marked with a jig. The rear clip line sits 34 mm lower | Clips not on a line, so springs twist | Spacing jig check |
| 11 | Install springs and clips | 12 sinuous 8-ga, 24 lined clips, 48 ring nails, 30 helical ties | Pneumatic clip nailer, spring stretcher | Spring centres ±(76.0, 192.8, 309.6, 426.4, 543.2, 660.0) mm from the centreline. Clip the front first, stretch to the rear clip. Arc crown 22 ±3 mm. Fit 3 rows of helical ties at 25/50/75 % span | Springs upside down (arc must face UP); missing liners | Every clip has a liner; crown measured at 4 springs |
| 12 | Inspect suspension | — | 10 kg weight, ruler | Deflection under a 10 kg disc, uniform ±3 mm across springs; no contact with wood under 100 kg (stretcher clearance ≥ 30 mm) | Squeak from tie hooks touching | Press test; listen for noise |
| 13 | Install protective decking | Felt insulator 300 g/m², 20 mm HR30 pad, decking cloth | Staple gun | Felt stapled to the rails (not to the springs), pad spray-glued on, decking cloth tensioned and stapled under the front rail | Stapling through springs | Smooth deck, no spring outline |
| 14 | Cut and shape foam | HR40, HR35, HR30 blocks | Bandknife, CNC contour cutter, hot-wire for the lumbar | Seat core 665 × 479 × 114; comfort 665 × 479 × 25; back core tapered 135→100 with a 12 mm lumbar crown; shell foams from the flattened patterns | Cutting the seat core to cover size (the core must be under-size to allow for the wrap) | ±2 mm; density and IFD certificates attached |
| 15 | Prepare back and arm padding | 5 mm bending ply; firm and soft foam; 150 g/m² wadding | Spray gun, contour knife | Bending-ply skin glued + stapled to the ribs (50 mm pitch). Glue the 20 mm firm foam, then 22 mm soft; round the rims with a knife; wadding last | Hard foam edge on the arm rim | Hand-feel along the rim: no ridge |
| 16 | Apply fibre wrapping | 200 g/m² bonded fibre | Spray tack | Wrap the seat foam top, front, back and underside; the domed top gets an extra 50 mm wide crown strip | Too little fibre, so the cushion looks "boxy" | Cushion crown 14–18 mm at the centre |
| 17 | Sew cushion covers | Bouclé, ticking, zips, thread Tex 70 | Industrial lockstitch, overlocker, cutting table | Seat cover 701 × 515 × 175, hidden side seam, 560 rear zip, corners eased. Back covers 701 × 520 × 185 with a bottom zip and hook-and-loop tabs. **All panels cut with the pile in the same direction** | Mixed pile direction looks like a colour change | Panels match colour under daylight |
| 18 | Sew outer upholstery panels | Bouclé | Lockstitch | Shell: outer-back + outer arms (one wrap), inside back, inside arms, 2 arm-front D-panels; the seam on the rim is pushed into the pile. Front base band as a tube | Seams on the sharp rim, which wear | Seam slippage test on an offcut |
| 19 | Upholster the arms and back | Shell cover | Staple gun, steamer | Fit the inside arms/back first, pull down to the deck and staple to the inner lower rail; outer wrap last, pulled under and stapled to the base stringers | Over-pulling bouclé causes loop distortion | No pleats on the arm fronts |
| 20 | Upholster the main body | Base band, decking | Staple gun | Band pulled over the bullnose foam, stapled under the front rail; ends tucked into the arm noses | Band ends visible at the arm junction | Band line straight ±2 mm |
| 21 | Fit the seat cushions | Seat and back inserts, covers | Cushion-stuffing machine (vacuum) | Inserts compressed into the covers; zips closed; back cushions fixed with hook-and-loop tabs | Inserts twisted inside the cover | Cushions sit level ±3 mm; equal gap at the centre |

## Cutting notes

- **Bouclé yield:** about 11–12 linear m at 140 cm width. Nest the arm-front D-panels and the band from offcuts. The pile direction always runs downwards on vertical panels and toward the front on the seat.
- **Ply nesting:** ribs, formers and panels fit on about 0.6 of a 2500 × 1250 sheet. Keep the face grain of the inner arm panels vertical.
